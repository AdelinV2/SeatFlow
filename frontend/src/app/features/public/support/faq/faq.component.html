Booking and holds

Choosing seats creates a temporary hold of up to 10 seats per reservation for 15 minutes. Availability is decided by the server: another customer may take the same seats first, and refreshing reconciles your view against the authoritative state. Details live in the Terms and Conditions.

Each reservation is limited to 10 seats. The limit is enforced by the server, not just the seat map interface. See Terms and Conditions and the security page for the booking-integrity summary.

Both paths use the same server-side booking rules. Guest checkout asks for a name and email and keeps that email in the same browser tab only so you can continue that checkout; an account keeps your tickets under My Digital Tickets after sign-in. How each path stores data is described in the privacy notice and the cookies and similar technologies page.

Tickets and access

Paid reservations produce tickets with a ticket code and QR access data. Anyone holding the ticket link or code can present it at the gate, so keep it private and do not post it publicly. Signed-in customers find their tickets under My Digital Tickets; guests use the ticket link or code issued at checkout in the same browser session. Ticket handling is also summarized in the Terms and Conditions.

Payments, tax, and refunds

No. The public portfolio deployment uses Stripe Test Mode: confirming payment with the provided test card completes a test transaction only and no real money moves. Card details are entered into Stripe Elements and are never stored by SeatFlow. See Tax and Test Payments.

Checkout shows the authoritative total for the held seats. Optionally entering a billing address asks Stripe Tax for a tax-inclusive breakdown of that total into tax and net portions; the preview is informational and never locks the final amount. SeatFlow is not a tax adviser and issues no invoices on this demo. Full detail: Tax and Test Payments.

Cancelling a pending hold releases the seats. A successful refund revokes the affected tickets and, when the session remains bookable, releases the seats so they can be booked again. A failed refund leaves the tickets valid. See the refund and cancellation policy.

Accounts and staff tools

Register or sign in with email and password, or with an enabled OAuth provider where offered. If you forget your password, use the password reset page to start recovery. Signing out ends the local session. Account data handling is described in the privacy notice.

The scanner is a role-protected staff capability for validating ticket codes at entry. It requires a staff role enforced by the server; it is not a customer support channel and signing in as a customer does not grant access. Entry decisions remain with the event organizer.

AI assistant and privacy

Where enabled and after sign-in, the assistant is optional and assistive: it can explain events and propose bookings through the same server rules as the rest of the site, using authoritative tool results for availability. It cannot bypass availability, holds, authorization, or payment confirmation, and it cannot create a payment on its own: only an explicit confirmation action finalizes a proposed booking. Conversations live in memory only; refreshing or signing out starts a new conversation, and prompts are still processed by the configured AI provider. If the assistant is unavailable, use event browsing and checkout directly.

Depending on how you use the site: account identity, booking and guest-checkout details, payment and ticket records, security and operational records, and assistant messages where the assistant is enabled. Browser storage behind these flows is itemized separately. See the privacy notice and the cookies and similar technologies page; the high-level protections are summarized on the security page.
